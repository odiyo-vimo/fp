How to use this page

  1. Read the summary, then work through the findings. Use the severity buttons to show High items first.
  2. Download the concept note v0.3 redraft from the bar at the top. It applies every finding and keeps your structure (sections 1 to 7), adding section 8 (timeline) and 9 (risks). Copy the parts you accept into your own v0.2.
  3. Use suggested wording for line-level edits if you prefer to edit your own file.
  4. Replace your diagram with the corrected pattern diagram. Download the .drawio file, or use Open diagram in diagrams.net to view and edit it in the browser (File, Save as, to keep a copy).
  5. Download the test and evidence catalogue. Close the decisions sheet first; then record Status, Run date, Tester and Evidence link per test. The Summary and Success criteria sheets update themselves. Yellow cells are for input.
  6. All files (.zip) holds every document from this repo, including the SDEC diagrams.

  • The note is short and the proposition is right: prove an operable service, not a file move. Keep that framing.
  • Three things would stop a reviewer signing it off as written: the diagram labels make Patterns 1 and 2 look like transfers into the same place, no runtime is named per pattern (so AV coverage for Pattern 1 is unknown), and the success criteria are not measurable.
  • Leftover text from the earlier single-feed candidate should go, and the in-scope items (CI/CD, security controls, negative testing) each need a matching success criterion.
  • Add a decisions table with owners, the missing dependencies, a short timeline and the top risks. All of this is in the suggested v0.3 redraft and the test catalogue.

Scope boundaries (suggested)

In scopeOut of scope for the MVP
  • All four transfer patterns (P1 to P4)
  • Scheduled and API-triggered transfer
  • Configuration, scheduling and promotion from dev to test
  • Security controls: TLS, credentials, IAM, encryption at rest, AV scanning
  • Monitoring, audit records and alerts
  • Negative-path testing (see test catalogue)
  • Draft runbook and support walk-through
  • Live or production data, and production go-live
  • Enterprise-wide migration and multiple business feeds
  • Transformation of file content
  • Performance, volume and soak testing
  • HA and DR failover testing
  • Payload encryption (PGP), unless brought in by D-MVP-10
  • Full ServiceNow or Dynatrace integration, unless essential to show operational readiness

5. Success criteria (revised)

Your four criteria are kept (SC-1, SC-2, SC-3, SC-5) and made measurable. SC-4, SC-6, SC-7 and SC-8 cover in-scope items that had no criterion. Targets are proposed and confirmed under D-MVP-07.

IDThe MVP is successful when...Origin
SC-1Each of the four patterns completes a scheduled and an API-triggered transfer, and the target SHA-256 matches the source.Original 1
SC-2Scheduled transfers run for at least five consecutive cycles per pattern with no manual intervention, and transfer history is visible end to end.Original 2
SC-3Non-arrival, transfer failure, AV detection and AV service failure each raise an alert to the agreed channel within the agreed time (target 15 minutes, D-MVP-07).Original 3, split
SC-4An infected, unscannable or oversize file is never delivered to any target.New
SC-5Every transfer creates an audit record with source, destination, file name, size, SHA-256, start and end timestamps, outcome and correlation ID.Original 4
SC-6Configuration is promoted from dev to test through the agreed mechanism with no manual re-keying; rollback is demonstrated or the gap is recorded against G-13.New
SC-7Security controls are evidenced: TLS 1.2 or higher on every leg, credentials held outside configuration (or the G-14 interim control recorded), least-privilege IAM on S3, encryption at rest.New
SC-8The support team can restart a failed transfer, replay a transfer and investigate a quarantined file using the draft runbook, without platform team help.New

Definition of done (suggested)

The MVP is complete when SC-1 to SC-8 are met, or any unmet criterion has an accepted gap with an owner; the evidence pack is signed off by the Boomi Platform Owner, Security Architect and Service Management Lead; and an adoption recommendation, including the position on G-13, G-14 and G-15, is presented to the Design Authority.
